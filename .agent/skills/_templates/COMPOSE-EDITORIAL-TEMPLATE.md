# Editorial composition (template)

Adapt this scaffold to the publication and audience. Preserve source meaning and the user's
chosen voice/angle; drafting does not authorize publishing.

## When to use

Newsletter editions, articles or case-study narratives where editorial angle and source fidelity
matter. Simple wording repair belongs to the project's writing skill.

## Approach

1. Establish audience, purpose, source material and requested degree of editorial collaboration.
   Distinguish sourced claims, inference and unsupported gaps. Ask only about gaps that change
   the result; do not invent findings or certainty to complete a narrative.
2. If the angle is unresolved, compare meaningfully different takes with the central claim,
   decisive evidence, audience value and risk. Use only as many alternatives as clarify the choice.
   A supplied/settled angle or delegated editorial choice does not need another pick-one gate.
3. For requested human-in-the-loop composition, get the consequential angle choice before building
   dependent sections. Offer headline/summary variants only where they resolve an actual choice.
   Preserve accepted sections; do not restart the whole process after a local revision.
4. Assemble the requested output. Check narrative continuity, attribution, claim strength, voice,
   headings and call to action against the brief.
5. Deliver the draft or requested comparison. Iterate on the feedback given. Publication is a
   distinct action requiring its own applicable grant.

## Verification / Definition of done

The output answers the brief, preserves source qualifications and accepted editorial decisions,
and contains no unsupported factual invention. Missing verification remains explicit.
Do not claim a draft is approved or published unless that actually happened.

## Output

The requested content or comparison in its destination format. Preserve the user's requested review
boundary without requiring a particular final phrase or an endless approval loop.
